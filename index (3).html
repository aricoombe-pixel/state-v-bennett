The closed universe of law

What the State has to prove

Bennett is charged with the agreement, not the robbery itself. The State does not need to prove Bennett robbed anyone. It has to prove all four elements below beyond a reasonable doubt, using direct or circumstantial evidence, which count equally.

An agreement

Bennett entered into an agreement with another person, here Morgan Pierce.

To commit armed robbery

The agreement's goal was robbery with a dangerous weapon.

Specific intent

Bennett and Pierce both intended that one of them would commit robbery with a dangerous weapon.

Knowledge

Bennett knew their actions were illegal.

Robbery = the goal
  • Intentionally takes property
  • From another person or organization
  • By force or threat of force
  • Intending to permanently deprive the owner

A dangerous weapon is anything capable of causing death or serious bodily harm. The parties stipulate a baseball bat qualifies.

Corroboration rule

Conspiracy can't be proven only with statements between the alleged co-conspirators. There must be independent evidence, such as specific acts or signs they were preparing together.

Burden-shifting

The State can never suggest Bennett had to disprove anything. If the Defense pushes its own theory, the State may point out the lack of evidence for it, without implying a legal duty. Object under Rule 403 or simply "burden-shifting."

Odd but true: conspiracy is always a misdemeanor in Maryland, even when the goal is a felony. The maximum penalty mirrors the target crime, so Bennett faces up to 20 years. Sentencing is off-limits at trial (Rule 401).

Memorandum & Order · binding at trial

Rulings, stipulations, special instructions

Motion 1 · Denied

Exclude the experts

Both Wade (law enforcement) and Barry (psychology) testify. Qualify them on direct, opponent may voir dire, then the court admits them. You can still object under 702(b)/(c) when they stray outside their field.

Motion 2 · Denied

Exclude Pierce's conduct

Pierce's actions are relevant and highly probative. But no "guilt by association." Bennett's guilt must rest on Bennett's own knowing participation.

Motion 3 · Denied (for now)

Exclude Pierce's statements

Co-conspirator statements come in conditionally under 801(d)(2)(E). The Defense must object when each statement comes up, then renew it specifically right before the Defense rests. One attorney renews. Vague renewals fail. The State must show a conspiracy by a preponderance.

Motion 4 · Granted

No "consciousness of guilt"

Nobody may use Bennett's request for a lawyer at the June 13 interview as evidence of guilt. The attorney crossing the witness who raises it objects under 403.

Special instructions

Witness selection

State must call Pierce and Wade, plus Cooper or Shaffer. Defense must call Barry and Bennett, plus Caldwell or Dawn. Three each, any order. Exchange witnesses, order and pronouns 48 business hours before trial; State discloses first. Miss the deadline and the defaults are Cooper and Dawn.

Courtroom

Wade is the State's party representative and Bennett sits at counsel table. Everyone else can be constructively excluded, which means pretending you weren't in the room, not actually leaving.

Witness rules

Witnesses must own their affidavits and signatures. No Fifth Amendment refusals. No implying a witness lacks normal intelligence or has a condition affecting memory (cross on perception and memory is still fine).

Exhibits & law

Exhibit 3(a) is the physical flash drive and 3(b) is its photo; either may be used, and nobody may imply investigators failed to bring it. No case citations. Use "the law requires…" in openings and closings, never "the Memorandum."

Case theory

How each side tells it

THE STATE

"An inside job with a game for a cover story."

  1. Motive. Bennett hated Shaffer for taking Bagley's idea, owed $300K, and said Shaffer "deserved to lose TotalSense."
  2. Means. Bennett suggested the update, picked the 4-minute window, set the code, and owned the keycard Pierce used.
  3. Plan. Mustang Tavern napkin, "stick to the plan," the Lucky 8 and the bat-swing gesture.
  4. Slip. June 4: Pierce mentions the demo and Bennett snaps "shut up."
  5. Payoff. OmniSense appears the next day: "What goes around, comes around."
THE DEFENSE

"Pierce did it, and Shaffer pointed at Bennett."

  1. Pierce's motive. Cameron's bills, "one way or another," a 2019 theft, and Shaffer's offer to "make that happen."
  2. Code elsewhere. "Orpheus" sat in a folder on Shaffer's desk and was a public game code.
  3. Napkin. CodeBlue strategy, confirmed by an outside player.
  4. Pierce's word. Bought with a suspended sentence after Davenport-style pressure.
  5. Victim. Bennett was sent down and hit with the bat. Sloppy, biased investigation; footage never pulled.

Personal knowledge602Witness is guessing. One witness's firsthand testimony is enough; no second witness needed.
Prior conviction609Felony within 10 years, or any dishonesty crime (Cooper's misrepresentation). Shaffer's PWID, Pierce's theft.
Leading611(c)Your opponent suggests answers on direct. Leading is fine on cross.
Narrative / compound / argumentative611Long story answers, two questions at once, badgering.
Refresh memory612Witness forgets; show the affidavit silently, take it back, re-ask.
Lay opinion701Dawn or Caldwell on handwriting; opinions must rest on their own perception.
Expert scope702 · 704(b)Expert strays outside their field; Barry can't say what Bennett intended.
Hearsay801–802Out-of-court statement offered for its truth. Not hearsay if offered to show it was said (an offer, a threat, effect on listener).
Party / co-conspirator801(d)(2)Bennett's own statements come in against Bennett. Pierce's come in under (E) if a conspiracy is shown.
State of mind803(3)Motive, intent, plan at the time ("I have a plan to pay those bills").
Business records803(6)Exhibits 4, 6, 14, 18 are already stipulated.
Handwriting / voice ID901Familiarity not gained for the lawsuit. Dawn's voice-chat ID of Bennett and Pierce.
Invention of factRule 9No objection allowed. Impeach with the affidavit instead.
Cross-examination drills

Practice on the stand

Read the question out loud, answer, then reveal the best answer. Short and true wins.

Kai Dawn

You told Bennett's lawyers you hoped your testimony would be "juicy" enough to get you to a million subscribers, correct?
Best: "I said that. I'm under oath, and I'm only telling you what I saw." Avoid: a bare "yes" that leaves the bias hanging.
You never saw the napkin in Exhibit 8 before this case, did you?
Best: "No, I hadn't." Short, no arguing.
On June 4, 2026, Pierce said they'd been "practicing," mentioned the Solara tech demo, and Bennett told Pierce to shut up. Correct?
Best: "Yes." Let redirect explain. Avoid: asking the attorney what time it was.
Pierce told you they needed to pay Cameron's bills "one way or another," didn't they?
Best: "Yes, Pierce said that." Avoid: snark. Judges score demeanor.
You say the Shaffer meeting was March 17, but Shaffer and Caldwell say March 20. You could be wrong?
Best: "My memory isn't perfect on dates, but I tied it to the St. Patrick's campaign." Avoid: "My memory isn't good" or claiming you called it an estimate.
You weren't at Solara on June 5, 2026, were you?
Best: "No, I wasn't."
You haven't spoken to either of them since June 4, so you don't know what they did on June 5?
Best: "Correct." Avoid: "I knew something was wrong," which is speculation that helps the State.
You stream PrimeFrame's game, and PrimeFrame's CEO is Bennett's mother. Your channel depends on her game, correct?
Best: "I stream on Clutch and CodeBlue is my main game. I don't know anything about Bennett's mother." Split compound questions.
You couldn't see Bennett's face over voice chat, so you can't know what "shut up" meant?
Best: "No." This one helps Bennett: it weakens the "sharper" tone.
Your opinion that Bennett is "laidback" rests on five months of talking over a game, even though Bennett mocked Cameron's cancer?
Best: "I met Bennett in person too. I only heard about the Cameron comment from Pierce. They argued in March, but by the next week they were friends again."

Aspen Caldwell

You now work for Jennifer Bagley, Bennett's mother, at nearly double your Solara pay?
Best: "Yes. She offered me the job after I was fired."
The door was closed and the heater drowned out part of the conversation, correct?
Best: "Parts were muffled, but I clearly heard 'I can make that happen. You just have to work with me.'"
You saw Pierce with a keycard on June 4, and Pierce said it was Bennett's?
Best: "Pierce said that. I don't know if it was true."
Bennett reported you and tried to get you fired, so you're angry at Bennett?
Best: "I was annoyed then, but Bennett was doing the right thing for security." This also helps the Defense.

Taylor Bennett

You chose a code anyone playing The Hades Company could find online as the code to the most secure room in the building?
Best: "Yes, it was on my mind. That was a mistake, but the code alone was useless without an admin keycard."
You told police "Shaffer deserved to lose TotalSense," didn't you?
Best: "I said that out of anger. Being angry isn't planning a robbery."
